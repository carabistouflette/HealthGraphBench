We examine when relational information and learned message passing improve prediction over simpler baselines in health-regulatory data. HealthGraphBench defines three temporal tasks: new MAUDE product--problem links, G--L deficiencies in US nursing-home inspections, and new Medicare Part D prescriber--drug links. We report retained benchmark results and two additional MAUDE analyses.

In the historical MAUDE test, neighbor frequency achieves Recall@10 of 0.192, compared with 0.173 for three-epoch GraphSAGE. A new analysis selected 30 epochs on 2023 validation and achieved pooled 2024--2025 Recall@10 of 0.211. This exceeds the historical point estimates but is not a matched three-versus-thirty-epoch test comparison. Reusing this mean-aggregation model, a follow-up comparison gives Recall@10 of 0.036 for a self-only model without message passing. The models have unequal active transformation capacity, and self-only training loss remains close to $\ln 2$; the contrast therefore does not isolate an aggregation effect.

On CMS, owner aggregates add 0.0013 to pooled AUC, with a historical interval including zero. On Part D, specialty popularity achieves Recall@10 of 0.218 versus 0.181 for BPR. Because evaluation periods had already been examined during development, the additional MAUDE results remain exploratory, without new confidence intervals or independent confirmation. The resource supports task-specific comparisons, not general graph superiority or clinical utility.
