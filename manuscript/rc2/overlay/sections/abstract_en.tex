We present three temporal health-regulatory tasks and compare relational heuristics with learned models: new product--problem links in MAUDE, G--L deficiencies in US nursing-home inspections, and new prescriber--drug links in Medicare Part D. A complementary MAUDE exploration examines sensitivity to training duration and removal of neighborhood aggregation.

In the historical MAUDE benchmark, neighbor frequency achieves Recall@10 of 0.192, compared with 0.173 for three-epoch GraphSAGE. The 0/3/10/30 validation grid is non-monotonic and selects 30 epochs on 2023. The selected model reaches 0.211 on 2024--2025. Its paired comparison with retained neighbor predictions gives a difference of $+0.018597$, with a conditional 95\,\% CI of $[+0.011975;+0.025570]$, obtained by resampling products. This result shows that the historical ordering does not describe the entire GraphSAGE family; it does not establish a causal effect of duration.

On CMS, owner aggregates increase pooled AUC by 0.0013, with a historical interval that includes zero. On Part D, specialty popularity achieves Recall@10 of 0.218, compared with 0.181 for BPR. The MAUDE control without message passing remains a secondary diagnostic under fixed settings, with nearly flat loss; it does not explain the change in ordering. Because the periods had already been examined, these analyses remain exploratory, and the CI covers neither selection nor training. Neither general graph superiority nor clinical utility is demonstrated.
