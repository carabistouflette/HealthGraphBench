We examine under what conditions relational information and message passing improve prediction compared with simpler baselines in health-regulatory data. HealthGraphBench defines three temporal tasks: new product--problem links in MAUDE, G--L deficiencies in US nursing-home inspections, and new prescriber--drug links in Medicare Part D. We present the benchmark's historical results and two complementary MAUDE analyses.

In the historical MAUDE test, neighbor frequency achieves Recall@10 of 0.192, compared with 0.173 for three-epoch GraphSAGE. A new analysis selects 30 epochs on 2023 validation and achieves pooled Recall@10 of 0.211 on 2024--2025. This exceeds the historical point estimates but is not a matched comparison of three and thirty epochs. A subsequent comparison reuses this mean-aggregation model and gives Recall@10 of 0.036 for a model limited to each node's own vector, without message passing. The models do not have the same active transformation capacity, and the control's training loss remains close to $\ln(2)$; this contrast therefore does not isolate the effect of aggregation.

On CMS, owner aggregates increase pooled AUC by 0.0013, with a historical interval that includes zero. On Part D, specialty popularity achieves Recall@10 of 0.218, compared with 0.181 for BPR. Because evaluation periods had already been examined during development, the new MAUDE analyses remain exploratory, without new confidence intervals or independent confirmation. These results demonstrate neither the general superiority of graphs nor clinical utility.
